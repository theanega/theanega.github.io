- Coding:
	- Python is not Spotify. You don't press the button and enjoy. You need to understand.
	- Read file paths twice before running the code. 
	- Test it first with one image/tumor/patient before running it for multiple. 
	- Autosave enabled always
	- Git is my friend
- You don't have to say yes. Focus on getting your thesis done. Yes, you have a choice. No, they won't be upset.
- Ask for help. Seek out collaborators and mentors. They don't have to from your group or from your institution. There are kind, brilliant people out there willing to help you. They're adults, if they're busy they'll tell you.
- Experiments:
	- You can tolerate ambiguity, you've done it before. There will always be uncertainty but you can still make decisions based on the best avaialble evidence (healthy skepticism with practical action! pragmatic idealist, not idealist!)
	- Computers don't volunteer information; you have to ask for it (https://blog.sbensu.com/posts/default-blind/). Very imprtant to stay optimisitc and approach the porject like "how can i Ifnd omehting good here?". Show resutls to someone before you decide there's nothing there. 
	- You're an imaging scientist, visualize the images, not just the arrays.
	- Get something done every day. Better to have bad results thatn have no results at all. Better to have output first than to have an optimized pipeline. If perfoamnce is an aissue after you have some result then optimize pipeline. https://old.reddit.com/r/dataengineering/comments/1dfmoyl/advice_from_senior_des_to_junior_des/.
